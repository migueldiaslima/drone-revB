\chapter{Retainers and integration}\label{sec:retainers}

Every component has a designed seat in the frame, and three small parts hold the ones a seat alone cannot: the receiver, the GPS module and the receiver antenna. All three are printed in TPU 95A HF. A rigid snap feature in PET-CF would have to deflect about \SI{0.6}{mm} over a few millimetres of wall, a root strain of about 6\,\% in a \SI{1.2}{mm} wall over \SI{4.1}{mm}, far beyond a short-fibre composite; the flexible polyurethane takes it, and it damps vibration on the components it holds. None of the retainers is part of the optimised structure. Their masses are inside the point masses of the model, and the capacitor on the power lead has no retainer: it floats on its soldered leads above the flight controller, with an insulating sleeve, so that no bend starts within \SI{2}{mm} of a solder joint.

Each retainer is generated by a script from the frame's own parameters and checked against every part and every component envelope: one closed solid, no interference, minimum clearances, and the intended interference where a press fit or a preload is designed. A TPU density of \SI{1.21}{g/cm^3} is assumed for the masses.

\section{Receiver cradle}\label{sec:ret-cradle}

The receiver board measures $18.4\times10.4$\,mm and, with components on both sides and heat-shrink, up to 4.0 to \SI{4.1}{mm} thick. Its cradle is a U section open at both ends, so the receiver wires and the antenna lead leave over the ends (\cref{fig:ret-parts}). The pocket is $19.0\times11.0$\,mm, \SI{0.3}{mm} larger than the board on each side. Snap lips along both side walls overlap the board edge by \SI{0.6}{mm}, with a \ang{45} lead-in, so the receiver presses in from above; the lip underside sits \SI{4.1}{mm} above the floor, and the TPU flexes for the thickest board. The lip on the antenna side is shortened to keep the antenna connector zone free, so the lead can be plugged after assembly. Low end stops, at or below the board's top face, locate it along its length.

The cradle has no fixing of its own. Two ears, \SI{1.4}{mm} thick, sit on two of the flight controller's screws, clamped between the anti-loosening nut and the nylon nut above it. The floor lies \SI{0.30}{mm} above the assumed envelope of the flight controller's components, which could not be measured on the board; at the first fit a sheet of paper must pass under the floor, and if it does not, a step in the floor is one parameter of the script. The cradle weighs \SI{0.88}{g} and clears the capacitor by \SI{1.75}{mm}, the tub by \SI{4.85}{mm} and the lid by \SI{10.7}{mm}.

\section{GPS cap}\label{sec:ret-gps}

The GPS module ($12\times17\times5$\,mm) sits in a pocket on the deck at the rear of the tub; the pocket rim locates it in $x$ and $y$, and the cap only holds it down. The cap is a plate \SI{0.8}{mm} thick, two extrusion lines, which flexes as a clamp, with a $9\times9$\,mm window over the ceramic antenna. Its underside sits \SI{0.3}{mm} below the module top, a preload that the plate's bending supplies; the clamp band around the window is \SI{4.0}{mm} wide at the ends and \SI{1.5}{mm} at the sides.

Two legs of \SI{5.4}{mm} diameter stand on the deck's cap bosses, and below each leg a press pin of \SI{3.325}{mm} diameter and \SI{4.0}{mm} length enters the \SI{3.2}{mm} hole of the boss: a diametral interference of \SI{0.125}{mm}, a press fit proven in TPU, with a \SI{0.4}{mm} lead-in chamfer. The cap therefore needs no screws and no inserts, and the holes stay at the insert size, so a screwed cap remains a fallback. It weighs \SI{0.58}{g}. Two dimensions depend on the module version and are checked when it arrives: a module \SI{4.5}{mm} tall turns the preload into a gap, corrected by one parameter, and the antenna patch must be centred and at least $9\times9$\,mm.

\section{Antenna holder}\label{sec:ret-antenna}

The receiver's T antenna is held straight under the keel, with the moulded junction vertical and the stem up, directly below the cable exit, so the coax enters the stem without a bend; inside the keel the cable follows a channel with one bend of radius \SI{7}{mm}, more than six times its diameter. The legs lie along $y$, \SI{5.32}{mm} from the rear arms in plan and \SI{6.1}{mm} above the landing plane at the holder's lowest point.

The holder clips around both legs with C-clips that wrap \ang{277}: the bore is \SI{0.2}{mm} larger than the \SI{1.4}{mm} leg, and the opening of \SI{1.06}{mm}, 0.76 times the leg diameter, lets the leg snap in from below and holds it. The junction snaps into a pocket from below. One M2 screw fixes the holder to an insert in the antenna pad of the tub, with \SI{3.5}{mm} of thread engagement, and a peg in a locating hole stops rotation. The holder weighs \SI{0.94}{g}; with the antenna, screw and insert, \SI{2.24}{g} act on the pad as one point mass in the model. The antenna placement is the standard arrangement for a dipole; its radio performance was not tested.

\section{Integration}\label{sec:ret-integration}

The assembly follows the thirteen steps around which the design space was drawn (\cref{sec:arch-designspace}). Three details close it. The power lead passes out through a front window of $11\times7$\,mm with its connector already fitted, and the connector stands on a tab at the front of the lid, so the battery plugs in from above. The GPS cable runs from the pocket down a channel inside the keel into the bay, with enough slack that the lid can be lifted and laid aside. The flight controller is mounted rotated from its default orientation, with the USB port on the $-y$ side and the battery pads forward, which the flight software corrects with its board-alignment setting and a motor remap, checked with the props removed.

A fit-test print of the tub, lid and four arms in PLA+ was assembled before the PET-CF parts (\cref{fig:ret-fittest}): the arm forks seat on the tub fins, and the lid closes on the tub rim with the GPS deck behind it.
